# Chapter 4

# Writing Style Guide

---

# Purpose

This chapter defines how BRAINS writes.

The Editorial Constitution defined what we believe.

The Psychology chapter defined why people read.

The Voice chapter defined how BRAINS sounds.

This chapter defines the mechanics of writing.

It establishes a consistent writing system that applies to every publication regardless of format.

Blogs.

LinkedIn.

X.

Newsletters.

Documentation.

Research reports.

Landing pages.

Whitepapers.

Product documentation.

Case studies.

Every written artifact follows these standards.

---

# The Purpose of Writing

Writing is not the transfer of information.

Writing is the transfer of understanding.

The objective is not to explain everything.

The objective is to improve the reader's ability to think.

Readers should never finish an article simply knowing more.

They should finish understanding more.

Knowledge informs.

Understanding transforms.

BRAINS always optimizes for understanding.

---

# Writing Principles

Every publication follows seven principles.

Clarity.

Precision.

Structure.

Flow.

Evidence.

Depth.

Readability.

Whenever these principles conflict with style, style loses.

Understanding is always the priority.

---

# Clarity Comes First

The purpose of writing is clarity.

Never attempt to sound intelligent.

Attempt to make intelligence accessible.

If a sentence can be simplified without losing meaning, simplify it.

If a paragraph can be reduced without losing value, reduce it.

If a word can be replaced with a simpler alternative, replace it.

Readers should understand difficult ideas without effort.

---

# Simplicity Is Not Simplicity of Thought

Simple writing does not mean shallow thinking.

It means organized thinking.

Complex ideas deserve simple explanations.

The more advanced the concept, the greater the responsibility to explain it clearly.

Experts simplify.

Amateurs complicate.

---

# One Idea Per Paragraph

Every paragraph should communicate one central idea.

Avoid combining unrelated concepts.

Poor

Validation matters.

Founders also struggle with hiring.

Pricing is difficult.

Better

Validation reduces uncertainty before execution.

Separate paragraph.

Hiring introduces organizational complexity.

Separate paragraph.

Pricing influences positioning and market perception.

Separate paragraph.

Readers process information sequentially.

Respect that process.

---

# Sentence Length

Prefer short sentences.

Mix short and medium-length sentences to create rhythm.

Avoid long compound sentences unless absolutely necessary.

Average sentence length should remain between twelve and twenty words.

Very short sentences can create emphasis.

Example.

Evidence changes decisions.

Long sentences should only exist when they improve precision.

Never because they sound impressive.

---

# Paragraph Length

Readers increasingly consume content on mobile devices.

Large paragraphs reduce readability.

Default paragraph length:

One to four sentences.

Longer paragraphs are acceptable inside research reports when required for technical explanations.

Whitespace is a design tool.

Use it intentionally.

---

# Every Sentence Must Earn Its Place

Remove every sentence that does not improve understanding.

Avoid introductions that delay value.

Avoid transitions that repeat previous ideas.

Avoid conclusions that simply summarize.

Each sentence should satisfy at least one purpose.

Introduce a concept.

Expand a concept.

Support a claim.

Present evidence.

Challenge an assumption.

Create curiosity.

Provide an example.

Strengthen a framework.

If a sentence performs none of these functions, remove it.

---

# Avoid Empty Language

Poor writing contains filler.

Examples include:

It is important to note.

Needless to say.

In today's rapidly changing world.

Without a doubt.

As everyone knows.

At the end of the day.

These phrases communicate almost nothing.

Delete them.

---

# Strong Verbs

Choose precise verbs.

Weak

Helps.

Improves.

Supports.

Works.

Better

Clarifies.

Validates.

Measures.

Identifies.

Reveals.

Accelerates.

Strengthens.

Improves should only be used when nothing more precise exists.

---

# Specific Beats General

General

Talk to customers.

Specific

Interview ten potential customers solving the problem manually today.

General

Validate your market.

Specific

Identify evidence that customers already spend money solving the problem.

Specific language creates credibility.

---

# Show Reasoning

Never jump directly to conclusions.

Reveal the thinking.

Poor

Founders should validate first.

Better

Building before validation increases the cost of learning because software becomes emotionally difficult to abandon.

Readers trust reasoning more than conclusions.

---

# Use Progressive Logic

Every section should naturally lead into the next.

Ask continuously:

What question does this paragraph create?

Answer it in the following paragraph.

Writing should feel inevitable.

Readers should never wonder why a section exists.

---

# Headings

Headings should communicate ideas.

Not labels.

Poor

Overview

Background

Summary

Better

Why Validation Usually Fails

The Cost of Building Too Early

Evidence Changes Everything

Headings should create curiosity while remaining accurate.

---

# Subheadings

Subheadings should organize thinking.

Not decorate pages.

Every H3 should expand the H2.

Avoid unnecessary nesting.

Readers should immediately understand the relationship between headings.

---

# Lists

Lists improve readability.

However, excessive lists reduce depth.

Use lists when:

Presenting frameworks.

Summarizing principles.

Comparing alternatives.

Presenting processes.

Avoid lists for ideas that deserve explanation.

Frameworks should always be explained.

Not merely listed.

---

# Frameworks

Frameworks are one of the defining characteristics of BRAINS.

Every major publication should introduce one memorable framework.

Good frameworks are:

Simple.

Visual.

Reusable.

Memorable.

Actionable.

Frameworks should become part of founder vocabulary.

Readers should remember the framework name months after reading the article.

---

# Analogies

Analogies simplify complexity.

Use them carefully.

Good analogy.

Technical debt resembles financial debt.

Interest accumulates over time.

Poor analogy.

Random comparisons created only for entertainment.

Every analogy should improve understanding.

---

# Examples

Every important concept should include examples whenever possible.

Examples should be:

Real.

Believable.

Relevant.

Practical.

Use startup examples.

Product examples.

Engineering examples.

Historical examples.

Decision examples.

Avoid fictional stories unless clearly identified.

---

# Stories

Stories exist to explain principles.

Not emotions.

Every story should answer one question.

What decision mattered?

Stories should remain concise.

The lesson always matters more than the narrative.

---

# Numbers

Use numbers carefully.

Never invent statistics.

When numbers are unavailable, explain patterns.

Instead of creating artificial precision, acknowledge uncertainty.

Credibility matters more than numerical detail.

---

# Quotes

Use quotes sparingly.

Quote only when the original wording provides unique value.

Never fill articles with famous quotes.

BRAINS should become quotable itself.

---

# Transitions

Transitions should feel invisible.

Avoid phrases such as:

Moving on.

Next.

Another thing.

Instead allow ideas to naturally connect.

Readers should experience flow rather than structure.

---

# Conclusions

Never summarize the article.

Elevate it.

A conclusion should leave readers thinking differently than when they began.

The final paragraph should introduce perspective.

Not repetition.

Readers should remember the ending.

---

# Calls to Action

BRAINS does not pressure readers.

Preferred actions include:

Continue learning.

Explore related research.

Apply the framework.

Test the idea.

Challenge assumptions.

Avoid aggressive promotional language.

Trust generates customers.

Customers rarely generate trust.

---

# Tone

Maintain consistency.

Calm.

Confident.

Evidence-driven.

Curious.

Respectful.

Professional.

Never sarcastic.

Never arrogant.

Never sensational.

---

# Readability

Aim for writing that is accessible to experienced professionals.

Readers should never need to reread paragraphs to understand basic ideas.

Technical depth is encouraged.

Unnecessary complexity is not.

---

# Grammar Standards

Use active voice whenever practical.

Prefer present tense unless historical context requires otherwise.

Avoid excessive passive constructions.

Avoid unnecessary adverbs.

Avoid excessive adjectives.

Strong nouns and verbs create stronger writing.

---

# Formatting Standards

Use whitespace generously.

Use headings consistently.

Keep paragraphs short.

Avoid giant blocks of text.

Avoid decorative formatting.

Formatting exists to improve reading.

Not appearance.

---

# Editorial Language Rules

Always define uncommon terminology.

Avoid unexplained acronyms.

Avoid startup jargon unless necessary.

Avoid corporate language.

Avoid motivational clichés.

Avoid internet slang.

Avoid excessive abbreviations.

Write for intelligent readers.

Not specialists only.

---

# Non-Negotiable Rules

Never use em dashes.

Never use emojis unless explicitly required by platform guidelines.

Never use excessive punctuation.

Never use clickbait headlines.

Never use exaggerated claims.

Never fabricate examples.

Never imitate another writer.

Never publish generic AI language.

---

# Final Validation Checklist

Before publishing, confirm the following.

Every paragraph teaches one idea.

Every sentence has purpose.

Every section builds logically.

Every heading communicates meaning.

Every example improves understanding.

Every framework is memorable.

Every conclusion changes perspective.

The article is concise.

The article is readable.

The article sounds like BRAINS.

There are absolutely no em dashes anywhere.

---

# Writing Philosophy

Good writing explains.

Great writing transforms.

BRAINS does not measure success by how much content it publishes.

BRAINS measures success by how much better founders think after reading it.

Every sentence should move readers toward better judgment.

That is the standard.

That is the responsibility.

That is the BRAINS writing system.
