# Chapter 6

# Blog Editorial Standard

---

# Purpose

A BRAINS article is not content.

It is research.

Every article should become a permanent asset of the company.

Traffic is temporary.

Knowledge compounds.

Every article should improve the intelligence of founders.

If an article cannot improve decision making, it should not exist.

---

# Our Philosophy

Most startup blogs exist to attract traffic.

BRAINS exists to build institutional knowledge.

Publishing is not success.

Changing how founders think is.

The objective of every article is to permanently improve the reader's judgment.

Readers should finish an article with a better mental model than when they started.

Information is cheap.

Judgment is scarce.

BRAINS publishes judgment.

---

# The Gold Standard

We do not benchmark ourselves against average startup blogs.

We benchmark ourselves against organizations that consistently produce high-quality thinking.

Examples include:

Y Combinator.

Paul Graham.

Stripe.

Linear.

OpenAI.

Anthropic.

37signals.

Cloudflare.

These organizations rarely publish.

But when they do, people read.

The lesson is obvious.

Publishing frequency does not create authority.

Consistent quality creates authority.

BRAINS should follow this principle.

---

# The BRAINS Difference

Y Combinator explains startups.

Paul Graham explains founders.

Stripe explains infrastructure.

Linear explains product development.

OpenAI explains research.

BRAINS explains decision making.

Every article should improve one decision.

Not one skill.

Not one feature.

One decision.

Examples.

Should I build this?

Should I pivot?

Should I interview customers?

Should I hire?

Should I raise funding?

Should I automate?

Should I launch?

Decision quality becomes the center of every publication.

---

# Every Article Must Have One Thesis

Every great article argues one central idea.

Never write around a topic.

Write around a thesis.

Poor.

Startup Validation

Good.

Most startup validation fails because founders attempt to confirm rather than discover.

Poor.

Product Roadmaps

Good.

Most roadmaps become feature collections because teams prioritize requests instead of learning.

Readers remember arguments.

Not subjects.

---

# Every Article Must Introduce Original Thinking

Originality is mandatory.

Never publish an article that simply summarizes existing knowledge.

Readers should encounter at least one idea they have never considered.

One framework.

One observation.

One mental model.

One analogy.

One principle.

One contradiction.

One insight.

If an article introduces nothing new, rewrite it.

---

# Every Article Should Feel Like Research

The structure of a BRAINS article resembles research.

Observation.

↓

Question.

↓

Investigation.

↓

Evidence.

↓

Pattern.

↓

Framework.

↓

Application.

↓

Conclusion.

Never begin with recommendations.

Understand before advising.

---

# The Reader Journey

Every article should guide readers through predictable psychological stages.

Attention.

Curiosity.

Recognition.

Understanding.

Insight.

Confidence.

Action.

Memory.

Trust.

If one stage disappears, engagement decreases.

Readers should never feel lost.

---

# The Anatomy of a BRAINS Article

Every article follows the same editorial architecture.

## 1. Title

One idea.

Clear.

Specific.

No clickbait.

Readers should immediately understand the promise.

Examples.

Why Startup Validation Usually Fails

The Hidden Cost of Building Too Early

Why Founders Misinterpret Customer Feedback

Bad titles optimize curiosity.

Great titles optimize relevance.

---

## 2. Opening

The first paragraph determines whether readers continue.

Never waste it.

Never begin with history.

Never begin with definitions.

Never begin with generic context.

Instead begin with:

An observation.

A contradiction.

A surprising pattern.

A difficult question.

A founder mistake.

An unexpected insight.

Example.

Most founders think validation fails because customers reject the product.

In reality validation often fails long before the first customer interview.

Immediately readers ask why.

Curiosity begins.

---

## 3. Context

After creating curiosity, explain why the problem matters.

Readers should understand:

Who experiences this problem?

Why does it happen?

Why is it expensive?

Why should they continue reading?

Never assume importance.

Explain it.

---

## 4. Root Cause

This is where average articles stop.

BRAINS begins here.

Do not explain symptoms.

Explain causes.

Ask repeatedly.

Why?

Why again?

Why again?

Continue until the underlying decision problem appears.

Readers remember causes.

Not symptoms.

---

## 5. Evidence

Evidence creates trust.

Evidence may include:

Research.

Founder interviews.

Historical examples.

Observed patterns.

Product analytics.

Industry reports.

Customer conversations.

Internal platform intelligence.

Never rely on opinion when evidence exists.

---

## 6. Framework

Every major article should introduce one memorable framework.

Frameworks become intellectual property.

Examples.

Evidence Pyramid.

Decision Confidence Matrix.

Validation Ladder.

Execution Debt.

Learning Velocity Curve.

Frameworks should satisfy five requirements.

Simple.

Visual.

Reusable.

Memorable.

Practical.

Readers should remember framework names months later.

---

## 7. Application

Understanding alone changes little.

Application changes behavior.

Readers should leave knowing:

What should I do tomorrow?

What should I stop doing?

What should I measure?

What should I question?

What experiment should I run?

Every article should produce action.

---

## 8. Closing

Never summarize.

Elevate.

The ending should leave readers with a new perspective.

Good endings continue thinking after reading ends.

# Article Length

Different ideas require different lengths.

Never write to reach a word count.

Write until understanding is complete.

Recommended ranges.

Quick Insight

800 to 1,200 words

Framework

1,500 to 2,500 words

Deep Analysis

2,500 to 4,000 words

Research Report

4,000 to 8,000 words

Foundational Guide

5,000 to 10,000 words

Length follows complexity.

Never the opposite.

---

# Headings

Headings should answer questions.

Not organize documents.

Poor.

Background

Overview

Conclusion

Better.

Why Validation Fails

The Hidden Cost of False Confidence

Evidence Changes Everything

Readers should understand the article simply by reading headings.

---

# FAQs

Most websites misuse FAQs.

They answer obvious questions.

Google already answers obvious questions.

BRAINS FAQs answer second-order questions.

For every major article include **4 to 8 FAQs**.

Each FAQ should answer a question the reader naturally asks after finishing the article.

Example.

Article:

Why Startup Validation Usually Fails

FAQs.

How many customer interviews are enough?

What if customers disagree?

Should I build before validating?

How do I measure validation quality?

Can AI replace customer interviews?

How long should validation take?

FAQs extend understanding.

They do not repeat the article.

---

# Visual Thinking

Readers understand faster through structure.

Whenever possible include:

Decision trees.

Matrices.

Flow diagrams.

Tables.

Process maps.

Framework illustrations.

Comparison charts.

Visuals should simplify.

Never decorate.

# Research Requirements

Every article begins with research.

Minimum inputs before writing.

Ten to twenty reputable sources.

Founder interviews where available.

Industry reports.

Academic research when relevant.

Internal BRAINS knowledge.

Original observations.

Writing begins only after patterns emerge.

# The 70-20-10 Rule

A BRAINS article should contain approximately:

70 percent timeless thinking.

20 percent current industry context.

10 percent opinion.

This creates evergreen value while remaining relevant.

# SEO Philosophy

SEO is a distribution channel.

Not the purpose.

Never damage readability for keywords.

Never repeat phrases unnaturally.

Write for founders.

Search engines increasingly reward articles that founders genuinely value.

User satisfaction is the best SEO strategy.

# Internal Linking

Every article should strengthen the BRAINS knowledge graph.

Every publication should naturally reference related frameworks.

Related research.

Supporting articles.

Product workflows.

Case studies.

Knowledge should become interconnected.

Readers should naturally continue learning.

---

# Editorial Review

Before publication every article should satisfy these questions.

Does it introduce an original insight?

Does it improve founder judgment?

Does it explain root causes?

Does it include evidence?

Does it introduce a memorable framework?

Would an experienced founder save it?

Would a YC partner consider it thoughtful?

Would a senior PM forward it internally?

Would it still matter in five years?

If any answer is no, continue improving.

---

# The Five-Year Rule

Before publishing ask:

Will this article still be valuable five years from today?

If not, ask why.

Sometimes short-lived articles are necessary.

Most should become timeless references.

Build a library.

Not a news feed.

---

# The BRAINS Editorial Promise

Every article should leave the reader with three things.

One better question.

One stronger framework.

One decision they will never make the same way again.

That is the standard.

Anything less is simply another blog post.

BRAINS does not publish blog posts.

BRAINS publishes startup intelligence.

Never use the rhetorical pattern:

"Don't X. Do Y."

"Not X. Y."

"Good founders don't just X. They Y."

"Success isn't X. It's Y."

"A isn't about B. It's about C."

These constructions have become predictable AI writing patterns.

Instead:

• State the insight directly.
• Explain the reasoning.
• Let the reader arrive at the conclusion.
• Use observations instead of rhetorical contrasts.
