# BRAINS Master Article Template

Version: 1.0

Status: Production

Classification: Canonical Knowledge Object

---

# Purpose

Every article produced by BRAINS is a knowledge asset.

The objective is not to publish content.

The objective is to improve founder decision making.

Every article should teach a reusable way of thinking.

Every article should introduce at least one memorable framework.

Every article should strengthen the BRAINS Knowledge Graph.

---

# Article Length

Default target.

2,000 to 3,500 words.

Never write to reach a word count.

Write until the problem is completely explained.

Longer articles are acceptable when additional depth genuinely improves understanding.

Shorter articles are acceptable only when they fully answer the reader's question.

---

# Target Audience

Primary.

Founders.

Product Managers.

Software Engineers.

UI and UX Designers.

Technical Leaders.

Startup Operators.

Investors.

Decision Makers.

Assume the reader is intelligent.

Never oversimplify.

---

# Article Structure

Every article follows the same progression.

---

# 1.

## Title

The title should explain.

Not tease.

Readers should immediately understand what they will learn.

Good.

How Startup Validation Actually Works

Bad.

Everything You Know About Validation Is Wrong

---

# 2.

## Executive Summary

Maximum.

100 words.

Answer.

What is this article about?

Why does it matter?

Who should read it?

What will they learn?

This section exists for busy readers.

---

# 3.

## The Problem

Start with the real founder problem.

Not history.

Not storytelling.

Not definitions.

Describe.

Why this problem exists.

Who experiences it.

Why it matters.

The cost of misunderstanding it.

The reader should immediately recognise themselves.

---

# 4.

## The Context

Explain the environment.

What has changed?

Why is this topic important now?

What misconceptions exist?

Challenge assumptions early.

---

# 5.

## Research & Evidence

Present observations.

Research.

Data.

Examples.

Case studies.

Founder experiences.

Product experiences.

Avoid unsupported opinions.

Every claim should have reasoning.

---

# 6.

## Pattern Recognition

Move beyond isolated observations.

Identify recurring patterns.

Explain why they happen.

Patterns create understanding.

---

# 7.

## The BRAINS Framework

Every article should introduce one primary framework.

Explain.

Purpose.

Components.

Relationships.

Advantages.

Trade-offs.

Visual representation.

Examples.

Failure cases.

Frameworks are the intellectual property of BRAINS.

---

# 8.

## Application

Show readers exactly how to use the framework.

Step by step.

Real founder examples.

Real product examples.

Real decision making.

Theory becomes practice.

---

# 9.

## Common Mistakes

Address misconceptions.

Incorrect assumptions.

Typical failures.

Explain why they happen.

Explain how to avoid them.

Readers should learn from mistakes before making them.

---

# 10.

## Limitations

Every framework has limitations.

Discuss them honestly.

Transparency builds trust.

Never present frameworks as universally correct.

---

# 11.

## Related Concepts

Connect this article to the BRAINS Knowledge Graph.

Reference.

Related frameworks.

Related articles.

Related research.

Related templates.

Knowledge should compound.

---

# 12.

## Key Takeaways

Five to ten concise insights.

Not a summary.

A reusable reference.

Readers should be able to screenshot this section.

---

# 13.

## Action Plan

Explain what readers should do next.

Concrete actions.

Not motivation.

Decision making.

Learning.

Experiments.

Implementation.

---
