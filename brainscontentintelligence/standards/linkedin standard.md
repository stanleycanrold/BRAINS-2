# Chapter 7

# LinkedIn Editorial Standard

---

# Purpose

LinkedIn is not a social network.

It is the largest professional attention marketplace in the world.

Founders.

Investors.

Product managers.

Engineers.

Designers.

Operators.

Executives.

Researchers.

Recruiters.

Customers.

Partners.

Every important startup decision maker spends time on LinkedIn.

Most companies use LinkedIn to promote themselves.

BRAINS uses LinkedIn to improve how founders think.

This distinction changes everything.

We are not building followers.

We are building intellectual authority.

Every LinkedIn post should contribute to that objective.

---

# The Mission

The mission of LinkedIn content is not engagement.

It is recognition.

Recognition that BRAINS consistently publishes ideas worth stopping for.

Eventually, founders should see the first two lines of a BRAINS post and stop scrolling without seeing the author.

That is the standard.

---

# The LinkedIn Philosophy

LinkedIn rewards conversations.

Not advertisements.

People do not share advertisements.

They share ideas.

They share observations.

They share experiences.

They share frameworks.

They share new ways of thinking.

Every BRAINS post should introduce one meaningful idea.

Not multiple.

One.

Readers remember one idea.

They forget ten.

---

# What Makes High-Performing LinkedIn Content

After studying high-performing technical founders, operators, investors, and startup educators, several consistent patterns emerge.

The best posts do not chase algorithms.

They understand psychology.

They consistently create four reactions.

"I never thought about that."

"I've experienced that."

"I should save this."

"I need to send this to someone."

Every BRAINS post should aim to create at least one of these reactions.

---

# What BRAINS Will Never Become

BRAINS will never become an influencer brand.

We are building an institutional brand.

Influencers sell personality.

BRAINS builds intellectual property.

Influencers depend on visibility.

BRAINS depends on trust.

Influencers compete for attention.

BRAINS competes for understanding.

Attention follows understanding.

Not the opposite.

---

# The BRAINS LinkedIn Position

Every post should position BRAINS as one of four things.

A researcher.

A builder.

A teacher.

An operator.

Never as a marketer.

---

# The Identity of Every Post

Every post should answer one question.

What should readers know after reading this that they did not know before?

If nothing changes in the reader's thinking, the post failed.

Knowledge transfer is the objective.

---

# The Four Categories of LinkedIn Posts

Every post belongs to one primary category.

## Research

Original observations.

Patterns.

Industry analysis.

Startup data.

Platform insights.

Research earns authority.

---

## Frameworks

Mental models.

Decision systems.

Operating principles.

Reusable thinking.

Frameworks create memory.

---

## Stories

Experiences.

Customer interactions.

Founder mistakes.

Lessons learned.

Stories create emotion.

Stories exist to teach principles.

Not entertain.

---

## Contrarian Thinking

Challenge common startup beliefs.

Only when evidence supports the challenge.

Contrarian thinking creates discussion.

Not controversy.

---

# The BRAINS Content Pyramid

Not every post has the same purpose.

Approximately.

40% Frameworks.

25% Research.

20% Stories.

10% AI.

5% Company updates.

Most companies reverse this ratio.

They mostly talk about themselves.

BRAINS mostly talks about ideas.

Ideas attract people.

People eventually discover products.

---

# The Three Second Rule

A LinkedIn post has approximately three seconds to earn attention.

The first sentence determines everything.

Never waste it.

Never begin with:

"I've been thinking..."

"Today I want to talk about..."

"In this post..."

Nobody cares.

Instead begin with:

A contradiction.

An uncomfortable truth.

An observation.

A surprising statistic.

A founder mistake.

A difficult question.

An unexpected conclusion.

Examples.

Most startup validation fails before founders speak to a single customer.

Your roadmap probably measures activity instead of learning.

Founders rarely fail because they build too little.

They fail because they build too confidently.

These openings interrupt scrolling.

---

# The First Line Formula

The first line should create one question inside the reader's mind.

How?

Why?

Really?

What do they mean?

Without curiosity, reading stops.

---

# The Second Line

The second line should increase curiosity.

Not satisfy it.

Example.

Most startup validation fails before founders speak to customers.

Because founders ask questions designed to confirm their ideas.

Readers continue because they need the explanation.

---

# The Third Line

The third line transitions into understanding.

Curiosity.

↓

Explanation.

↓

Evidence.

↓

Framework.

↓

Action.

This sequence appears repeatedly across successful educational content.

---

# Every Post Needs One Insight

One post.

One insight.

Never combine unrelated lessons.

Bad.

Validation.

Hiring.

Fundraising.

AI.

Leadership.

One post.

Good.

One deep lesson about validation.

Readers remember focused thinking.

---

# Every Post Should Challenge Something

People stop for novelty.

Not familiarity.

Every BRAINS post should challenge one assumption.

Examples.

Validation is not customer interviews.

Roadmaps do not create strategy.

Shipping faster rarely fixes poor decisions.

AI does not replace thinking.

Frameworks matter more than features.

Never manufacture disagreement.

Challenge assumptions responsibly.

---

# The Rule of Evidence

Never make bold claims without reasoning.

Every insight should answer.

Why?

How?

What evidence supports this?

Reasoning creates credibility.

Opinion alone does not.

---

# The BRAINS LinkedIn Structure

Most educational posts should follow this structure.

Observation.

↓

Problem.

↓

Why it happens.

↓

Framework.

↓

Application.

↓

Closing insight.

This structure creates natural progression.

---

# Formatting

LinkedIn is consumed quickly.

Formatting improves readability.

Use short paragraphs.

One to three sentences.

Strategic whitespace.

Lists when appropriate.

Avoid giant blocks of text.

Avoid decorative formatting.

Formatting exists to reduce cognitive effort.

---

# The Length Standard

Different ideas require different lengths.

Short Observation

100 to 200 words.

Framework

250 to 500 words.

Deep Insight

500 to 900 words.

Long-form LinkedIn Article

1,200 to 2,500 words.

Never extend a post simply because longer posts perform well.

Write until the idea is complete.

---

# The Framework Rule

Frameworks create brand recognition.

Every week BRAINS should publish at least one original framework.

Examples.

Validation Pyramid.

Decision Confidence Matrix.

Founder Learning Loop.

Evidence Ladder.

Customer Signal Map.

Execution Debt.

Frameworks become memorable assets.

Lists become forgotten.

---

# AI Content

Most AI content is shallow.

BRAINS approaches AI differently.

Instead of:

"Five AI tools."

Write:

"How AI changes founder decision making."

Instead of:

"Best prompts."

Write:

"Prompt engineering for customer discovery."

Focus on principles.

Not tools.

---

# Storytelling

Stories should answer one question.

What decision mattered?

Avoid chronological storytelling.

Instead organize stories around insight.

Context.

Decision.

Mistake.

Learning.

Framework.

Application.

The lesson always matters more than the story.

---

# Calls to Action

Never beg for engagement.

Avoid.

Thoughts?

Agree?

Comment below.

Like and share.

Instead encourage thinking.

Examples.

The next customer interview you conduct should answer one assumption.

Evidence changes roadmaps faster than opinions.

Which assumption are you testing this week?

Calls to action should continue the conversation.

Not manipulate it.

---

# Comments

Comments are part of the content strategy.

Respond with curiosity.

Expand ideas.

Provide additional frameworks.

Answer thoughtfully.

Never argue emotionally.

Every comment is another opportunity to demonstrate expertise.

---

# Posting Frequency

Consistency matters.

Quality matters more.

Recommended cadence.

Three to five posts each week.

One deep framework.

One founder psychology post.

One AI insight.

One product thinking post.

One research observation.

Publishing less is acceptable.

Publishing weak content is not.

---

# The BRAINS Engagement Philosophy

Engagement is feedback.

Not success.

High engagement with weak thinking is failure.

Low engagement with excellent thinking is an opportunity to improve distribution.

Never compromise quality to increase engagement.

Authority compounds slowly.

Virality disappears quickly.

---

# The 5-Second Test

Before publishing ask.

Would I stop scrolling?

Would an experienced founder stop?

Would a senior PM save this?

Would a YC partner finish reading?

Would this still be valuable one year from today?

If not, continue improving.

---

# The BRAINS LinkedIn Score

Every post is scored from one to five.

Originality.

Clarity.

Evidence.

Memorability.

Practical value.

Conversation quality.

Trust.

Posts scoring below four should not be published.

---

# What Makes a World-Class LinkedIn Post

The world's best LinkedIn posts have one characteristic in common.

They change how readers think.

Not simply what readers know.

Readers forget information.

Readers remember perspective.

Every BRAINS post should introduce a perspective worth remembering.

---

# The Long-Term Vision

LinkedIn is not our destination.

It is a distribution channel.

Every post should direct readers toward the BRAINS knowledge ecosystem.

Blogs.

Research.

Frameworks.

Case studies.

Product workflows.

Newsletters.

The objective is not isolated posts.

The objective is building one connected library of startup intelligence.

Every LinkedIn post is an entry point into that library.

---

# The Editorial Promise

Readers should know exactly what happens when they see a BRAINS post.

They will learn something practical.

They will encounter a new mental model.

They will question one assumption.

They will become slightly better founders.

That expectation becomes our brand.

---

# Non-Negotiable Rules

Never use em dashes.

Never use clickbait.

Never fabricate stories.

Never invent statistics.

Never chase trends without insight.

Never post generic AI advice.

Never copy another creator.

Study great thinkers.

Understand their principles.

Develop the BRAINS perspective.

Original thinking is the competitive advantage.

---

# Closing Principle

LinkedIn rewards visibility.

BRAINS optimizes for credibility.

Visibility creates impressions.

Credibility creates trust.

Trust creates authority.

Authority creates category leadership.

That is the long-term objective.
